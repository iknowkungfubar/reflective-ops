# Synthetic End-to-End Example

> **Synthetic scenario:** The person and situation below are fictional.

## Concern

A learner intends to practice a new language most evenings but repeatedly postpones the session until it no longer happens.

## 1. Route

The orchestrator selects `behavior-forensics` because the primary problem is repeated avoidance of a chosen behavior.

## 2. Behavior forensics

Recent pattern:

- planned practice after dinner,
- opened the lesson,
- saw a long exercise set,
- checked entertainment content “for a minute,”
- did not return,
- immediate result: task pressure disappeared,
- delayed result: practice was missed.

Exception:

On days when the learner used a five-minute practice set, starting was easy.

### Hypotheses

**H1:** perceived task size creates initiation friction.  
**H2:** entertainment provides a stronger immediate reward regardless of task size.

## 3. Experiment

For six practice opportunities:

- prepare a five-minute lesson in advance,
- keep the entertainment device outside immediate reach until the five minutes are complete.

### Predictions

If H1 dominates, initiation should rise sharply with the smaller lesson.

If H2 dominates, avoidance may persist even with the smaller lesson unless the competing cue changes.

### Measure

- opportunities,
- starts,
- five-minute completions,
- optional continuation,
- reason for each miss.

## 4. Review

After six opportunities, compare actual starts with the predictions and update H1/H2 rather than creating a new motivational story.
