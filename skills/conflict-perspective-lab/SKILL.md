# Conflict Perspective Lab

## Objective

Prepare for a difficult conversation without mind-reading or reflexively taking one side.

## Invoke when

The user is:

- preparing for conflict,
- reviewing an argument,
- setting a boundary,
- making a complaint,
- attempting repair,
- trying to understand a misunderstanding.

## Four-layer model

Separate:

1. what the user intended,
2. what the user actually said or did,
3. what the other person could observe or hear,
4. what meaning either person assigned.

## Procedure

### 1. Reconstruct the user's side
Collect:
- goal,
- observable event,
- interpretation,
- proposed message,
- desired outcome,
- unacceptable outcome.

### 2. Neutral observer
Rewrite the event using only what an outside camera and microphone could capture.

### 3. Steelman
Construct the strongest reasonable version of the other person's position.

Label it explicitly as **hypothetical**.

### 4. Vulnerability audit
Identify:
- weakest part of the user's case,
- ambiguity,
- accusation disguised as observation,
- missing information,
- phrase likely to trigger defensiveness.

### 5. Possible needs
Generate two or three possible concerns the other person might have.

Label every one as a hypothesis.

### 6. Rehearsal
Prefer:
> observation → impact/experience → question or request

over:
> accusation → inferred motive → verdict

### 7. Optional role-play
If requested, simulate:
- cooperative,
- defensive,
- misunderstanding-prone.

The simulation is practice, not evidence.

## Output

- neutral reconstruction,
- strongest opposing case,
- weakest point in the user's case,
- likely ambiguity,
- revised opening,
- two or three questions,
- one boundary if needed,
- conditions for postponing the conversation.

## Mandatory evidence and safety rules

- Do not diagnose the user or a third party.
- Do not present generated psychological explanations as discovered facts.
- Do not invent hidden motives, intentions, memories, or feelings.
- Separate observations, interpretations, hypotheses, predictions, and unknowns.
- For material conclusions, consider at least one credible competing explanation.
- Identify evidence that would weaken the leading hypothesis.
- Preserve the user's decision authority.
- Do not copy user-specific personal information into reusable artifacts, examples, or public material.
- Treat examples in this skill as procedural illustrations, not templates for assuming facts about a user.
