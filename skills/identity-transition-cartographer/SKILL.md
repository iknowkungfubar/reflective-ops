# Identity Transition Cartographer

## Objective

Navigate a role or life transition without treating an old role as the person's permanent identity.

## Invoke when

The user is dealing with:

- career transition,
- retirement,
- relationship-role change,
- major life-stage change,
- loss of a former role,
- uncertainty about a new chapter.

## Separate

### Roles
Temporary positions or social roles.

### Capabilities
What the person can do.

### Patterns
Habitual ways of operating.

### Values
Chosen qualities or directions.

### Attachments
Status, recognition, certainty, routines, community.

### Constraints
Time, money, energy, obligations, access.

### Experiments
Low-cost ways to test a possible future.

## Interview

Ask one question at a time.

Useful questions:
- What did you value in the old role independent of its title?
- What did people reliably rely on you for?
- Which strengths become liabilities when overused?
- What are you trying to preserve?
- What are you willing to stop optimizing for?
- What future possibility could be tested without declaring a new identity?

## Transition map

### Carry
Transferable assets.

### Recalibrate
Strengths that need a different intensity or context.

### Leave
Practices or assumptions no longer worth their cost.

### Build
Missing capability, support, routine, evidence, or environment.

### Acknowledge
Real losses that should not be “optimized away.”

### Test
A short reversible experiment.

## Output

- old territory,
- possible new territory,
- bridge assets,
- bridge gaps,
- costs and risks,
- smallest real test,
- decision trigger after the test.

## Mandatory evidence and safety rules

- Do not diagnose the user or a third party.
- Do not present generated psychological explanations as discovered facts.
- Do not invent hidden motives, intentions, memories, or feelings.
- Separate observations, interpretations, hypotheses, predictions, and unknowns.
- For material conclusions, consider at least one credible competing explanation.
- Identify evidence that would weaken the leading hypothesis.
- Preserve the user's decision authority.
- Do not copy user-specific personal information into reusable artifacts, examples, or public material.
- Treat examples in this skill as procedural illustrations, not templates for assuming facts about a user.
