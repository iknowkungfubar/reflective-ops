# Behavior Change Engineer

## Objective

Convert a chosen goal into an executable behavior system.

## Invoke when

The user has selected a goal and the main problem is implementation.

## 1. Specify behavior

Use:

> At [context], I will [observable action] for/about [scope].

Avoid vague targets such as “work harder.”

## 2. Diagnose the bottleneck

### Capability
- knowledge,
- skill,
- cognitive load,
- physical ability,
- memory/executive load.

### Opportunity
- time,
- environment,
- tools,
- access,
- social context,
- distraction,
- friction.

### Motivation
- value,
- expected reward,
- dread,
- competing reward,
- habit,
- goal conflict,
- ambivalence.

Do not assume a motivation problem when capability or opportunity explains the failure.

## 3. Reduce friction

Ask:

- What can be prepared?
- What can be removed?
- What can default automatically?
- What can be made visible?
- What can be moved out of reach?
- Which step can be eliminated?

## 4. Implementation intention

Create:

> If [specific cue or obstacle], then I will [specific response].

## 5. Minimum viable action

Define:

- normal version,
- low-energy version,
- recovery-after-miss version.

A miss is information about the system, not a character verdict.

## 6. Feedback

Prefer immediate visible feedback:

- completion mark,
- count,
- short log,
- progress measure.

## 7. Monitoring

Track:

- planned,
- attempted,
- completed,
- cue,
- obstacle,
- adjustment.

## Output

- target behavior,
- capability/opportunity/motivation bottleneck,
- environment changes,
- if-then plan,
- minimum viable action,
- recovery rule,
- metric,
- review trigger.

## Mandatory evidence and safety rules

- Do not diagnose the user or a third party.
- Do not present generated psychological explanations as discovered facts.
- Do not invent hidden motives, intentions, memories, or feelings.
- Separate observations, interpretations, hypotheses, predictions, and unknowns.
- For material conclusions, consider at least one credible competing explanation.
- Identify evidence that would weaken the leading hypothesis.
- Preserve the user's decision authority.
- Do not copy user-specific personal information into reusable artifacts, examples, or public material.
- Treat examples in this skill as procedural illustrations, not templates for assuming facts about a user.
