# Research Basis

ReflectiveOps is **research-informed**, not clinically validated.

The sources below support mechanisms or design choices used by the skills. They do not establish that an AI agent is a substitute for a professional or that the whole ReflectiveOps system has been validated as an intervention.

## Implementation intentions

Implementation intentions specify a cue-response relationship in an if-then form. A 2024 meta-analysis covering 642 tests reported effects across cognitive, affective, and behavioral outcomes, with stronger results for contingent if-then formats under several conditions.

Keller et al. (2024), *The when and how of planning: Meta-analysis of the scope and components of implementation intentions in 642 tests.*

https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563

**Used in:** `behavior-change-engineer`

## Mental contrasting with implementation intentions

A 2021 meta-analysis of 21 studies and 15,907 participants reported a small-to-medium effect on goal attainment, while also identifying evidence of publication bias.

Wang, Wang & Gai (2021), *A Meta-Analysis of the Effects of Mental Contrasting With Implementation Intentions on Goal Attainment.*

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149892/

**Used as:** supporting background for converting goals into concrete action plans.

## Progress monitoring

A meta-analysis of 138 studies with 19,951 participants found that interventions increasing progress monitoring also improved goal attainment. Effects were larger when progress was physically recorded or reported.

Harkin et al. (2016), *Does monitoring goal progress promote goal attainment?*

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479070/

**Used in:** `review-calibration`, `behavior-change-engineer`

## COM-B

COM-B models behavior as depending on capability, opportunity, and motivation, helping avoid the mistake of treating every failure to act as a motivation problem.

Michie, van Stralen & West (2011), *The behaviour change wheel: a new method for characterising and designing behaviour change interventions.*

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21513547/

**Used in:** `behavior-change-engineer`

## Self-distancing

Research and meta-analytic work suggest that adopting a distanced observer perspective can reduce emotional intensity and support reflective processing, with effect sizes and contexts varying across studies.

Powers & LaBar (2019), *Regulating Emotion Through Distancing.*

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6326885/

Murdoch et al. (2023), *The effectiveness of self-distanced versus self-immersed reflections among adults.*

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36166459/

**Used in:** `conflict-perspective-lab`, `belief-reality-auditor`

## Conflict reappraisal

A study of 120 married couples reported that a brief conflict-reappraisal intervention was associated with preservation of marital quality during the second year of a longitudinal study.

Finkel et al. (2013), *A Brief Intervention to Promote Conflict Reappraisal Preserves Marital Quality Over Time.*

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23804960/

**Used as:** evidence for neutral-observer perspective taking, not as evidence that AI role-play can reveal a partner's internal state.

## Cognitive restructuring

A 2023 meta-analytic review found an association between observed cognitive restructuring and psychotherapy outcome, while the direct mechanism-analysis literature included in that review was relatively small.

Hundt et al. (2023), *Cognitive restructuring and psychotherapy outcome: A meta-analytic review.*

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10440210/

**Used as:** background for testing interpretations rather than accepting them automatically.

## Motivational interviewing

Motivational interviewing has evidence across multiple behavior-change contexts, but effectiveness varies by domain, setting, delivery, and study quality.

Rubak et al. (2005), systematic review/meta-analysis:

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1463134/

2024 BMJ systematic review of interventions containing motivational interviewing for adult physical activity:

https://www.bmj.com/content/386/bmj-2023-078713

**Used in:** `change-readiness-interviewer`

The skill borrows autonomy-supportive and ambivalence-exploration principles. It does not claim to deliver clinical motivational interviewing.

## Identity change and the end-of-history illusion

Research involving more than 19,000 participants found that people tend to recognize substantial past personal change while underestimating future change.

Quoidbach, Gilbert & Wilson (2013), *The End of History Illusion.*

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23288539/

**Used in:** `identity-transition-cartographer`

## Prospective hindsight and premortems

Prospective hindsight asks a decision maker to imagine an outcome as already having occurred and explain why.

Mitchell, Russo & Pennington (1989), *Back to the future: Temporal perspective in the explanation of events.*

https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bdm.3960020103

**Used in:** `decision-lab`

Premortems remain a heuristic and may introduce their own biases; they are not treated as forecasts.

## Real-options reasoning

Real-options reasoning supports staged or reversible commitments when uncertainty can be reduced before a larger irreversible decision.

Janney & Dess (2004), *Can real-options analysis improve decision-making? Promises and pitfalls.*

https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/AME.2004.15268687

**Used in:** `decision-lab`, `experiment-designer`

## Expressive writing: evidence limitation

Expressive writing is often presented as broadly therapeutic. Evidence is mixed. A randomized-trial meta-analysis reported minor or no general effects on somatic and psychological health outcomes.

Mogk et al. (2009), *Health effects of expressive writing on stressful or traumatic experiences — a meta-analysis.*

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736499/

**Design consequence:** `adversity-debrief` treats writing as an organizing tool, not a universal treatment.

## Self-compassion and self-criticism

A 2022 meta-analysis found reductions in self-criticism following self-compassion-related interventions, while noting limitations in study quality and heterogeneity.

Wakelin et al. (2022):

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33749936/

**Design consequence:** ReflectiveOps separates accurate self-correction from humiliation or character attack.

## AI mental-health-related safety

Professional and public-health guidance warns that general-purpose generative AI can hallucinate, over-agree, reinforce user perspectives, miss context, and behave unreliably in mental-health-related situations.

American Psychological Association (2026), health advisory:

https://www.apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-chatbots-wellness-apps

American Psychological Association (2026), guide to navigating AI-generated advice:

https://www.apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/guide-navigating-ai

World Health Organization (2026), responsible AI for mental health and well-being:

https://www.who.int/news/item/20-03-2026-towards-responsible-ai-for-mental-health-and-well-being--experts-chart-a-way-forward

**Used in:** all safety and counter-sycophancy rules.
